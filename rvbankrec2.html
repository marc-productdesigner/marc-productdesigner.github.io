Redesigning bank reconciliation from the ground up.

Bank reconciliation is one of the most critical — and most dreaded — tasks in property management accounting. The old flow required property managers to manually check off every transaction without any guidance, context, or intelligence. We set out to change that.

This project contains proprietary work. The details shared here are limited to what's publicly available. I'm happy to walk you through the full process in a conversation.

Problem

The original reconciliation tool asked property managers to check off transactions manually against a bank statement, with no assistance from the system. The layout split "Money In" and "Money Out" into two disconnected tables, buried the most important balance indicators in a sidebar, and offered no indication of which transactions actually matched or why.

The result: monthly close took significantly longer than it should, errors were common, and users lacked confidence that their reconciliation was accurate when they hit "Reconcile."

Before

The old screen required users to manually scan two separate transaction tables and check off items one by one. There was no matching logic, no confidence indicator, and no clear prioritization of what to review first.

Old reconciliation screen

Solution

Reconciliation 2.0 is a ground-up redesign of how property managers close their books. It introduces intelligent transaction matching, three distinct workflows to fit different team needs, and a new interface architecture that surfaces the most important information upfront.

New Interface Architecture

The biggest structural change was replacing the two disconnected tables (Money In / Money Out) with a unified transaction view, anchored by three key balance indicators at the top of every screen.

Reconciliation setup — workflow selection

The Triple-Tie Banner

Pinned to the top of every reconciliation session, it shows three balances that must all agree before a user can close out the period. When all three match, the account is tied out.

Ending Balance

The statement balance — what the bank says the account holds at period close.

Cleared Balance

The beginning balance plus all checked transactions in the reconciliation.

Adjusted Cash Balance

The RentVine ledger balance adjusted for outstanding items not yet cleared.

How it ties out

When all three numbers match, the user can reconcile. If they don't, the banner surfaces where to start.

Triple-Tie Banner

Match Confidence Tracker

Rather than dropping users into a wall of unchecked transactions, the new interface surfaces a confidence score for every line item. The tracker sorts transactions into three buckets and tells users exactly where to focus first.

Matched

High confidence. The system already checked these off. Spot-check a sample and move on.

Needs Review

Multiple possible matches, or a weaker signal. Open the Match Drawer to pick the right one.

No Match

No ledger entry found. This is where problems live. Start here, every time.

Transaction Table

Each row shows its match status at a glance. Users can open the Match Drawer on any row to see the full matching logic, review alternatives, or manually override the system's suggestion.

The Match Drawer

A panel that opens from any transaction row, showing the full matching context: the confidence logic used, suggested alternatives, and the ability to combine multiple ledger entries against one bank line.

Three Workflows

One of the key design decisions was supporting three distinct ways teams actually reconcile, rather than forcing everyone through one rigid flow.

Match Assist — Monthly statement upload

The team uploads a PDF bank statement, the system analyzes it, and Match Assist runs against the ledger. Best for teams doing monthly close.

1

Select the bank account and confirm Bank Sync is off

2

Upload the statement by drag-and-drop or file picker

3

Wait for the system to analyze and populate the Match Confidence Tracker

4

Review No Match items first, then Needs Review, then spot-check Matched

5

Click Reconcile when the Triple-Tie Banner agrees

Match Assist — statement upload

Bank Sync — Live connection via Plaid

For teams that want ongoing transaction visibility, Bank Sync connects directly to the bank through Plaid. Transactions pull in roughly twice a day and are matched as they arrive. RentVine never stores bank login credentials.

Manual

No upload, no live connection. Users check off transactions directly against their statement. The Match Confidence Tracker doesn't apply here, but the Triple-Tie Banner still guides completion.

How Matching Works

Step 1 — Rules-based: The system checks amount, date, check number, and transaction type. Deposits within 3 days match automatically. Payments within 5 days match. ACH transactions matching by settlement ID, amount within 5 cents, and date within 5 days match. When conditions are close but not exact, the item goes to Needs Review.

Step 2 — Intelligent matching: When rules aren't enough, the system evaluates description similarity and historical transaction patterns to suggest a likely match with a confidence score. Users can always override in the Match Drawer.

Outcomes

Reconciliation 2.0 shipped to all RentVine accounts in 2026. The redesign directly addresses the most common failure patterns from the old tool: no match visibility, no prioritization, and no system guidance on where to start.

  • Property managers now have a clear, prioritized review order for every reconciliation session
  • The Triple-Tie Banner eliminates the guesswork of knowing when a reconciliation is actually complete
  • Match Assist and Bank Sync reduce manual transaction checking for teams that previously had no automation
  • Split matching handles complex scenarios that the old tool couldn't address at all
